🔗 Building Thai Dialect Corpora through Reverse Dictionary: Benchmarks and Methods
View Abstract

We recast the reverse dictionary (RD) from a downstream lookup tool into an upstream corpus-construction pipeline for Thai dialects. Given free-form definitions, the pipeline retrieves dialectal headwords with metadata, produces ranked candidate lists, and supports semi-automatic human validation.

We instantiate this paradigm with multi-part benchmarks—Swadesh prompts, exact-match queries, and IDS-derived paraphrases—and evaluate hybrid dense retrieval (FAISS with character/lexical signals) alongside lightweight classifiers built on Thai and multilingual pretrained encoders.

Results show that hybrid embedding retrieval is robust to orthographic variation and OCR noise and provides the high recall needed for efficient corpus harvesting; adding reranking or small classifiers consistently lifts top-rank accuracy with minimal throughput cost.

Multilingual and dialect-aware encoders outperform models trained only on Standard Thai. The outcome is a reproducible RD-driven workflow for building usable Thai dialect corpora and a general recipe for other low-resource dialects, with implications for scalable data creation, dialect-aware NLP, and language preservation.
